# Dangers

[← Back to the beginner's guide](../README.md)

Space is not a safe place. This page covers what can go wrong, and how to be
ready for it. The **About** page shows how often things happen in your
universe.

## Pirates

Pirates are everywhere, and they come in several ways.

### Pirate raids on your planets

Once you're past newbie protection, pirates will attack your planets from time
to time.

- **You are warned well ahead.** A red card on the Overview and a countdown in
  the top bar show which planet is targeted and when they strike.
- **They match your planet.** Pirates bring a fleet sized to the ships and
  defenses on the targeted planet, so a stronger planet faces a stronger raid,
  but they still bring less than you have.
- **With a Deep Space Scanner** anywhere in your empire, you can see what the
  incoming fleet is made of.
- **After a raid you get a break** before the next one.

**What you can do:**

- Build defenses and a shield dome on the targeted planet.
- Move your fleet home to fight, or send it away if it can't win.
- Spend or ship out resources you don't want to lose.
- Ask clan members to **Defend** your planet with their ships.

### Ambushes on your fleets

- **On long flights**, pirates may jump a fleet somewhere along the way,
  without warning.
- **Parked at a foreign world**, a fleet may see pirates closing in. You are
  warned before they strike, so you can recall it or stand and fight.
- **Explorers charting a new world** may find pirates waiting there. A Deep
  Space Scanner on the planet they're sent from makes this rarer.

Pirates bring only a fraction of your fleet's strength, so an armed fleet
usually wins and takes a share of the pirates' wreckage. Unarmed fleets
(probes, cargo ships) are the ones at risk. Very small fleets aren't worth the
pirates' time.

### Pirate havens, bastions and stations

- A **pirate haven** is a world pirates hold. Defeat them to claim their hoard
  and settle the planet.
- A **pirate bastion** is an armed station hidden behind a signal. It sends
  raids at nearby empires until someone destroys it.
- **Space stations** in some systems are held by pirates behind heavy
  defenses and a hoard of treasure. Beat them, and you can settle the station.

## Illness

A planet with many people can suffer an **outbreak**, and expeditions
sometimes bring an illness home.

- **Quarantine.** While a planet is ill, no fleet can leave it, and the sick
  can't work, so buildings and defenses may run short-handed.
- **Waves.** Every few hours the illness spreads: some of the sick die, and
  more of the healthy fall ill. It ends by itself after a while, but at a high
  cost.
- **Heal it.** Build a **Hospital** and press **Heal** on the red outbreak card
  on the Overview. Healing costs water and food (sometimes other resources too)
  for every sick person, so heal early, while it's cheap. Higher Hospital
  levels heal faster and cheaper, and make outbreaks rarer.
- After an outbreak, the planet is safe from a new one for a while.

## Other empires

- **Players** can attack your planets, plunder your resources and invade your
  colonies (never your home world). Spy reports warn you when someone spied on
  you and got caught.
- **AI empires** (marked "AI") live in the galaxy too. They grow, trade, settle
  new worlds and fight each other. They also raid players nearby and spy
  before they strike, and if you attack them, they strike back soon after.
- **Newbie protection** keeps new players safe from attacks (by players and AI
  empires alike) until they've grown a little.

> **Tip:** Keep an eye on the **Radar** and the Overview. A Deep Space Scanner
> shows fleets flying near your planets, so you can prepare, or intercept them
> before they arrive.

## Risky adventures

- **Expeditions** into deep space can find treasure, but they can also meet
  pirates, get lost for a while, or never come back.
- **Black holes** are the biggest gamble: a fleet sent in may return rich, be
  thrown to a distant system, or vanish completely. Only send what you can
  afford to lose.

## Running your planets

Some dangers are quieter:

- **Hunger and overcrowding.** Without enough water and food, or with more
  people than room, a planet's population shrinks.
- **Missing workers.** Without enough people, buildings and defenses work
  weaker and new ships wait in dock without a crew.
- **No power.** Without enough energy, every mine slows down.
- **Full stores.** Resources that don't fit are lost. A landing fleet's extra
  metal and crystal stay in orbit as debris; extra deuterium and xenite are
  gone.
- **Old age** takes a small share of the civilians every week.

See [Your planets](planets.md) for how to keep everything running.
